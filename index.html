How does Folio work with Plex?+

Folio connects to an accessible Plex Media Server and reads audiobooks organized as albums in a music library. You can sign in with Plex or use a server URL and token. Your server needs HTTPS and must allow browser access. Folio is an independent project and is not affiliated with Plex.

Can I really listen without internet?+

Yes. Download the book while connected and wait for all tracks to finish. Downloaded audio and your place are stored in this browser on this device. Your browser must support the source audio format; MP3, AAC, and M4B support varies by browser and codec. Keep Folio open during downloads; browsers may pause transfers when the app is closed or your device sleeps.

Will my downloads stay on my device?+

Folio requests persistent storage where supported, but your browser or operating system may remove website data when storage is low. Clearing website data, using private browsing, or uninstalling can also remove downloads. Check the Downloads screen before traveling. Downloads are device-specific.

Does it do everything a native audiobook app does?+

Folio includes offline downloads, bookmarks and notes, chapters, speed controls, a sleep timer, a listening queue, and Plex progress updates. Available chapter metadata depends on your files and Plex library. Browser background playback and lock-screen controls depend on your device. Folio does not provide native CarPlay, Apple Watch, or Siri integration, automatic transcripts, or native background downloads.

What does Folio cost?+

Folio has no subscription or payments. You provide your own Plex server and audiobook files. Plex and any server hosting may have their own costs and terms.

Where are my library and listening data stored?+

Your connection details, bookmarks, queue, settings, downloads, and local progress are stored in your browser. Folio communicates directly with Plex and your server for sign-in, library access, playback, and progress updates. Use a device you trust and sign out on shared devices.
